# Game Design Foundations

## The core loop
Every game is a loop the player repeats: **observe → decide → act → get feedback → (reward or setback) → observe**. Design the smallest loop first (seconds long: jump, shoot, dodge), then the middle loop (minutes: clear a room, solve a puzzle), then the long loop (hours: unlock a region, upgrade, finish the story). If the 5-second loop is not fun on its own, nothing layered on top will save it.

## MDA: Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics
- **Mechanics** are the rules and verbs you write (move, jump, collect, health = 3).
- **Dynamics** are the behaviour that emerges when players use them (rushing, hoarding, risky shortcuts).
- **Aesthetics** are the feelings the player has (challenge, discovery, fantasy, expression).
Designers build from mechanics up; players experience from aesthetics down. Pick the target feeling first, then choose mechanics whose dynamics produce it.

## Verbs
A game's identity is its verbs. Give the player 2–4 primary verbs and make each one interact with the world in more than one way (a dash that also crosses gaps, breaks blocks and dodges damage). Depth comes from combinations of few verbs, not from many verbs.

## Player motivation
- **Competence**: clear goals, readable feedback, a difficulty that rises with skill.
- **Autonomy**: meaningful choices — routes, order of objectives, risk vs reward.
- **Curiosity**: secrets visible but not yet reachable; the map hinting at more.
Reward curiosity with something tangible (a collectible, a shortcut, lore).

## Goals and feedback
The player should always be able to answer: *What am I trying to do? What just happened? Was that good?* Every action needs an immediate, legible response — a sound, a flash, a number, a change in the world.

## Risk and reward
Interesting decisions put something valuable behind something dangerous. Tune so the safe path is viable and the risky path is tempting.
